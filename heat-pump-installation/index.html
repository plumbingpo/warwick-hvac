Many Warwick, RI homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are choosing a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, a genuine engineering leap over earlier generations.

Great heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Warwick HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

Installation follows the same discipline as every Warwick HVAC system: line set pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed with superheat and subcooling, the outdoor unit elevated on a stand where winter defrost drainage demands it, condensate handled properly, and the thermostat configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost behave the way the engineering intended. Then we commission in both modes, heating and cooling, and record the readings. Why Professional Heat Pump Installation Matters

Since a heat pump runs in both seasons, installation mistakes cost you twice. An incorrect charge cuts cooling efficiency in summer and heating capacity in winter. Skip the balance point calculation and electric backup strips may end up heating the house at several times the operating cost, leaving the homeowner puzzled about why the bills never dropped. Leave the thermostat on factory defaults and it can call for backup heat the compressor could have handled, or interfere with defrost. Set the outdoor unit flat on the ground in a real winter and it sits in refrozen defrost water until the coil turns to ice.

Our experienced technicians design and install against all of it: loads calculated for both seasons, equipment chosen for the actual capacity curve at your winter design temperature, backup heat staged and locked out correctly, the unit elevated for defrost drainage, and the charge weighed and verified rather than guessed. Commissioning runs in both modes, including confirming the reversing valve and watching a defrost cycle complete. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and inverter-driven equipment adds electronics that reward a careful setup. Heat Pump Installation Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
  • Check your first winter bill, since an unexpected jump often points to overworked backup heat
  • Rinse the outdoor coil each spring so summer performance starts clean
  • Keep a regular filter schedule so the system has enough air in summer and winter
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
  • Expect light frost in winter and a periodic defrost hiss; solid ice means call us
  • Avoid deep thermostat setbacks, which can call on costly backup heat during recovery
