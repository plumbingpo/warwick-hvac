From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Warwick homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Rather than guess, Warwick HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Warwick

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

At Warwick HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
